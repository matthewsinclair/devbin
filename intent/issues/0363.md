## What happened

Prodinfra reported this on 2026-11-15 (relayed by devbin-vc). Where two domains in a profile share a first label, eg `example.me` and `example.com`, `infra init` keys each by its whole name with the dots dashed, `example-me` and `example-com`: `infra_init_handles` in lib/infra.init gives both the dashed whole name rather than giving the second a suffix, so no handle records the order the domains were named in. Every `infra` verb that loads the profile, `check` among them, then validates it and prints, under "infra: handles worth a second look (not an error):", the line "handle `example-me` does not look derived from `example.me`", and the same for `example-com`. `infra_handle_matches_name` in lib/infra accepts a handle that is the first label, or one the FQDN contains, and a dashed whole name is neither. Reproduced at main on 2026-11-17 from the two functions alone: both dashed handles are flagged, and a lone domain's first-label handle is accepted.

## Why it matters

The advisory exists to catch a handle that was not derived from its domain, and it flags the derivation devbin's own `init` chose. A profile `init` wrote, unedited, draws it on every run, so the operator learns to read past the one block meant to catch a real mismatch. It is a warning and never refuses, which is why it is low.

## The fix

The check accepts the dashed whole name as a derivation, `${fqdn//./-}` in the report's words: the FQDN lowercased with each character outside `[a-z0-9_-]` dashed, as `infra_init_handles` writes it. One function gives that form to both `init` and the check, so the two cannot drift apart again. Filed for 0.1.27 on vc's decision 205.
