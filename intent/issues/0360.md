Filed for the weigh of the release after next (vc's todo 214). Record only, with no work now. Laksa's critic review of its runners found it, reading lib/runlog as 0.1.25 ships it; lib/runlog is unchanged at main since v0.1.25.

1. A run whose stdout and stderr both go to a pipe is left unsealed by SIGTERM to its group. When the caller sends both streams to one pipe (`2>&1 | tee`), the signal kills that pipe's reader too. `run_gate` notes the TERM and waits for its command, but before it reaches `trap '' PIPE` (lib/runlog:2432) bash writes the job's "Terminated: 15" notice to the dead stderr, and the shell dies of SIGPIPE. The run exits 141, the in-flight marker stays as the seal, no verdict or ledger row is written, and `$log.progress` is left beside the log. The critic reproduced it 3 of 3 (faithful/r1 to r3-rg-ign-both-TERM: status 141, the .progress file still there). With stderr to a file and only stdout piped, the same signal seals the run red with 143 (faithful/r1-rg-ign-pipe-TERM). The fix to weigh: ignore SIGPIPE from the moment a signal is noted, or before the gated command starts, rather than only after it ends.

2. Lower. `run_gate` called directly under a caller's errexit, outside `all`, exits 1 on the first failed write after a signal, when its stdout has no reader. The seal is written, but the run gets no verdict line, no ledger row, no traps_restore and no re-send of the signal. Under `all`, each gate runs in a subshell and the caller goes on (p1.sh and p2.sh show the two shapes; p4-all.sh and p4-gate.sh, the re-send).
