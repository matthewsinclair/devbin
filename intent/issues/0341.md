devbin 0.1.23's third cut on 2026-11-12 (release commit c13c101a, reset by hv before its tag) stopped red at its legs step. The local leg's `test all` read 3120 ok and 1 not ok. The not ok was releasecut.bats's "a macOS stage whose run fails is red with its exit, the newest record's word and its last line, and the cut stops at the legs step with nothing tagged (ST0011 WP-06)". Its body passed. Its teardown's `rm -rf "$DEVBIN_TEST_TMPDIR"` (devbin_teardown, tests/lib/test_helper.bash) failed with `Directory not empty`. The same arm passed in that morning's first cut and in 0.1.22's cut. rhadamanth's load average read 151 over 5 minutes, on 16 CPUs, at the end of that leg.

What wrote into the arm's temp dir after its body returned was the fixture's fake host near, not the product:

- legs_project's cut has a remote leg on near. fake_ssh (tests/lib/release_fixtures.bash) runs a host's half on this machine, with $HOSTS/near, inside the arm's temp dir, as its HOME.
- The leg's start (_release_leg_remote_start, lib/release.legs) starts the gates detached, as it does on a real host, so the run outlives the ssh that started it.
- The arm's macOS stage fails at once. The cut's stop (legs_stop, lib/cmd/release) stops and reaps the local gates and the stage, and leaves legs.near to finish on its host, by design, its seal read by a later run.
- So the arm can end while near's run still writes under $HOSTS/near. Under that load it outlived the arm and wrote while teardown removed the directory.

The red's own temp dir is the evidence. What rm left held only near's exit status, hosts/near/work/demo-leg-1699115fbce5.rc ("1"), and a re-created hosts/near/work/demo-leg-1699115fbce5/tmp/release/20261112-0919.GATES.errors, both stamped 09:19:42Z.

The product leaves no process of its own writing into the project after a red stop. The local gates and the macOS stage are stopped by process group and reaped before the step fails. A remote leg runs on its own host's disk, and its seal is copied back only when a later read asks for it. So a reset straight after a red stop is unaffected.

The fix belongs in the fixture: every run a fake host started has ended before an arm's temp dir is removed. releasecut.bats's teardown waits, to a deadline, for the exit status each such run writes as its last act, and fails the arm, naming any run still going at the deadline.
